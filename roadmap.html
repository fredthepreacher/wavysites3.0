Client success roadmap

The build system behind every Wavy Sites project.

This roadmap turns an objective into a working system: business discovery, architecture, premium design, development, working forms, SEO/AEO/GEO, deployment, QA, and post-launch growth — whether the end result is a website, custom software, automation, or all three.

Roadmap

1. Discover 2. Architect 3. Strategy 4. Design 5. Build 6. SEO/AEO/GEO 7. Forms 8. QA & Launch 9. Growth

Phase 01

Business discovery first.

Every project starts with the business problem, not the platform. Before design or development begins, identify the client's customer, offer, location, proof, conversion goal, and what must be true for a visitor — or a team member using internal software — to trust the system.

Business name, industry, service area, and customer type
Primary conversion goal and secondary conversion goal
Services, products, packages, or operational workflow
Assets, logos, photos, videos, reviews, and external links

Phase 02

Decide what actually needs to get built.

Not every objective needs the same answer. This phase turns the discovery notes into a scoped plan: a website, a custom software system, automation, or some combination — and what each piece needs to do before any screen gets designed.

Website, software, automation, or a combined build — scoped explicitly
Core data model and user roles for anything beyond a static site
Integration points: payments, CRM, scheduling, or existing tools
What must be true for launch, and what can come after

Phase 03

Turn notes into a customer or user journey.

A website should guide visitors through a clear path: understand, trust, explore, decide, and convert. Software should guide the person using it through their actual workflow just as clearly. Every screen should answer a real question for a real user.

Hero message or primary screen explains the system in five seconds
Services or features are grouped by how people actually look for them
Proof appears before the final call to action
Navigation stays simple and outcome-focused

Phase 04

Design for trust, not clutter.

The result should feel premium, clean, readable, and appropriate to the industry — whether it's a public-facing site or an internal dashboard. Images must be sharp, screens must breathe, and mobile layout must look intentional everywhere.

Strong hierarchy, consistent buttons, and visual rhythm
Readable contrast and tap-friendly spacing
No awkward cropping, broken assets, or duplicated sections
Brand colors, logo, photos, and proof used with purpose

Phase 05

Choose the simplest stack that fully supports the job.

Static HTML/CSS/JS is perfect for lean service sites. React/Next.js is used for reusable components, customer portals, and app-like interactions. Full-stack builds — with a real database, auth, and integrations like Stripe — are for CRMs, dashboards, and internal software.

Preserve working structure before rebuilding
Use reusable components or patterns where they help
Keep assets, data, and integrations organized and deployment-safe
Avoid unnecessary complexity for simple projects — and under-building real ones

Phase 06

Build for search, AI answers, and local visibility.

Every public-facing launch should include technical SEO, answer-ready content, local service-area language, structured data, sitemap, robots, image alt text, and useful FAQ content — so both search engines and AI answer engines understand what the business does.

Title tags, meta descriptions, H1/H2 hierarchy
LocalBusiness, Service, FAQ, Organization, or Product schema when appropriate
Service area and location language without keyword stuffing
Sitemap, robots file, Open Graph, and descriptive alt text

Phase 07

Forms and workflows must work, not just look good.

Quote and contact forms need correct markup, labels, required fields, a spam trap, and a real thank-you page. Software workflows need the same rigor — validated inputs, clear error states, and confirmation at every step. After deployment, every form and flow gets a live test.

Netlify-ready method, form name, hidden form-name, and honeypot
Accessible labels and reasonable required fields
Thank-you redirect or confirmation state included in the project
Live submission verified after deployment

Phase 08

Test before the client sees it.

QA protects the brand and the business. Check desktop, mobile, links, images, forms, thank-you pages, console errors, SEO tags, sitemap, robots, and deployment settings — and for software, real data flows, permissions, and edge cases.

No horizontal overflow or broken mobile navigation
All links, CTAs, images, forms, and workflows tested
No placeholder text, fake claims, or unverified reviews
Build root, publish directory, and domain setup confirmed

Phase 09

Turn launch into ongoing growth.

After launch, the website or system becomes the hub for Google Business updates, social posts, before/after content, landing pages, review graphics, SEO expansion, and monthly reporting — plus, for software, feature requests and usage-driven improvements.

Google Business Profile and local directory consistency
Before/after and testimonial content plan
Landing page and Google Ads readiness
Monthly maintenance, reporting, and SEO improvements

The roadmap is the operating system: business strategy, architecture, premium design, working forms, SEO/AEO/GEO, deployment, QA, handoff, and growth.
